The philosophy

The mile you forgot to log is the deduction you don't get.

The IRS doesn't take your word for it — they want a contemporaneous record. Written down close to when the trip happened, not reconstructed from memory in April.

The Mileage Log gives you a simple, honest place to log every trip the same day you drive it — so by tax season, the math is already done.

Questions

Answers first.

Is this the same as the Field Ledger?

No — they're two separate Google Sheets, sold separately. The Field Ledger tracks your overall Schedule C income and expenses. The Mileage Log is a dedicated, standalone sheet just for trip-by-trip mileage. They're built to work well together, but you don't need one to use the other.

Do I need to know how to use Google Sheets?

Not really. If you can type a date and an odometer reading into a box, you can use the Mileage Log. Everything is already labeled and formatted — no formulas to write, no setup from scratch.

Will this calculate my deduction for me?

It calculates your total business miles and multiplies them by the current IRS standard mileage rate, which is built in and editable if the rate changes. I'm not your tax preparer — the final deduction claimed on your return is between you and your preparer or tax software.

What counts as a business mile?

Generally, driving between job sites, to client meetings, supply runs, and similar work-related trips. Your regular commute from home to a fixed workplace typically doesn't count — the sheet includes a business/personal toggle to help keep that distinction clear, but always confirm specifics with your tax preparer.

Is there a subscription or renewal fee?

No. You get access once, make a copy to your own Google Drive, and it's yours permanently. No monthly charge, no annual renewal, no expiration date.

Can I share it with my tax preparer?

Yes. You can share the Google Sheet directly with your preparer as view-only access, or download it as an Excel file to send. Either way works.
