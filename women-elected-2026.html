Women Elected in 2026 — Their Plans & Platforms

Who won, where, and what each woman actually campaigned on — every woman who won a U.S. Senate or governor's race, plus all 36 women newly elected to the U.S. House, with a spotlight on South Carolina.

The big themes

Two very different playbooks won on Tuesday. Here's what each side's women winners ran on, drawn from their campaigns.

Democratic winners

  • Affordability: housing, childcare, energy bills, and repealing tariffs blamed for raising prices
  • Healthcare: restore Affordable Care Act subsidies, reverse the 2025 Medicaid cuts, lower drug prices — with a split between Medicare for All and a public option
  • Abortion rights: restore Roe, protect contraception and IVF
  • Anti-corruption: ban congressional stock trading and corporate PAC money — the single most repeated promise among new House members
  • Data centers: moratoriums or strict conditions so growth doesn't raise residents' power and water costs
  • Several democratic-socialist-aligned newcomers also ran on Medicare for All and abolishing ICE

Republican winners

  • Tax cuts: lower state and federal taxes, including deductions on tips, overtime, and Social Security
  • Border & immigration: tougher enforcement, expanded cooperation with federal immigration authorities, no sanctuary policies
  • Energy: expand coal, oil, gas, and nuclear production; faster permitting
  • Education: school choice and vouchers, parents' rights, local control
  • Pro-life positions and defense of the Second Amendment
  • Public safety: backing law enforcement, tougher bail and drunk-driving laws

U.S. Senate — 7 women won

At least 23 women will serve in the new Senate, down from 27 (Center for American Women and Politics). Two more women's races — Alaska and Mississippi — were still uncalled on Nov. 5.

Governors — 15 women won

Six incumbents re-elected, eight won open seats, and one — Gina Hinojosa of Texas — defeated a sitting governor. With two women not up for election this year, a record 17 women will serve as governors in 2027.

U.S. House — 36 women newly elected

New members of the 120th Congress: 33 first-time members plus three returning former members (marked † — new to this Congress, not first-time members of Congress). Overall, CAWP and CNN projected on Nov. 9 that at least 136 women will serve in the House — a record — though a few races were still uncalled. Two non-voting delegates were also newly elected women: Alicia Limtiaco (D, Guam) and Janelle Sarauw (D, U.S. Virgin Islands).

South Carolina spotlight

Two women won federal races in South Carolina — including a historic Senate first — while the governorship went to a man. Figures are unofficial until the State Election Commission certifies; AP called SC-01 on Nov. 6 after county certification.

Governor: Alan Wilson (R) — no woman winner

Wilson, the state's attorney general, won with 1,050,190 votes (51.6%) over Jermaine Johnson (D, 47.1%). AP called the race at 12:35 a.m. on Nov. 4. He succeeds term-limited Gov. Henry McMaster.

U.S. Senate: Annie Andrews (D) ★

Andrews won ~51.1% to ~46.7% over appointed Sen. Darline Graham (R) in a woman-vs.-woman race — becoming the first woman elected to the U.S. Senate from South Carolina. Her full platform is in her Senate card above.

U.S. House — all seven districts

  • SC-01: Nancy Lacore (D) — new woman winner. Beat Jenny Honeycutt (R) 49.65%–48.64% (~3,300 votes), a margin that triggered no recount. AP called the race on Nov. 6 after county certification, and her 1.01-point margin held. Outgoing Rep. Nancy Mace congratulated her on Nov. 4. Platform in her House card above.
  • SC-02: Joe Wilson (R), incumbent re-elected
  • SC-03: Sheri Biggs (R) — woman incumbent re-elected (not a new member)
  • SC-04: William Timmons (R), incumbent re-elected
  • SC-05: Wes Climer (R), new member, won the open Norman seat
  • SC-06: James Clyburn (D), incumbent re-elected
  • SC-07: Russell Fry (R), incumbent re-elected

Still uncalled on Nov. 9 — not counted above

As of the latest reports on Nov. 9, these were still uncalled. If any resolve for the woman, the totals above change. Resolved since Nov. 5: NY-17 (Cait Conley) and SC-01 (Nancy Lacore) were called by AP; WA-04 was called for Amanda McKinney (R), now counted above; and in AZ-05, Elizabeth Lee (D) lost to Mark Lamb (R) — not elected.

  • Senate, Alaska: Mary Peltola (D) led Sen. Dan Sullivan (R) by roughly 600 votes in AP's count on Nov. 8, with about a quarter of ballots still to be counted. Ranked-choice tabulation is scheduled Nov. 18. The race is also tangled in a ballot controversy over a second candidate named Dan Sullivan.
  • Senate, Mississippi: Scott Colom (D) led Sen. Cindy Hyde-Smith (R) by roughly 2,500 votes (49.1%–48.8%). Mississippi has no recount procedure; counties' certified results were due Nov. 13.
  • Governor, Kansas: Cindy Holscher (D) led Ty Masterson (R) by about 7,600 votes (roughly 0.7 point) after the Nov. 6 mail-ballot deadline. Provisional ballots (state deadline Nov. 10) and a mandatory close-race audit were still pending, Masterson had not conceded, and AP had not called it. Holscher declared victory Nov. 4.
  • Governor, Alaska: Bernadette Wilson (R) trailed Jonathan Kreiss-Tomkins (D) by about 20 points in first-choice votes (roughly 29% to 49%) with about 74% counted; a preliminary ranked-choice tally showed 55%–45%. Tabulation is set for Nov. 18, with certification due Nov. 25. I found no AP call; one aggregator (MultiState) lists Kreiss-Tomkins as the winner while NBC still listed it too early to call — a discrepancy, noted not resolved.
  • Louisiana runoffs (Dec. 12): Pat Moore (D, LA-05) and Chauna Banks (D, LA-06) advanced to runoffs — not elected on Nov. 3.
